# SF map exploration design notes

This records the selected SF map's integration into the incumbent main app, plus its exploration history. The current homepage uses brown elevation bands, green park elevation, a runtime daily-high gradient and fifteen readings, prioritizing the nearest predefined sample when real browser location is inside the map. The separate revision 7 combination board remains a synthetic archive. Earlier sections describe that study's historical constraints, not the current live-data integration. This is an ordinary extension of the existing visual system, not a new product-wide identity.

## Selected-map integration: current main app

[SITE-INTEGRATION.md](SITE-INTEGRATION.md) records implementation and verification. `src/components/RegionalMap.tsx` and its scoped `regional-map.css` place the selected map after the homepage header and before forecasts through `src/App.tsx`; regional detail routes retain their existing forecast view. `src/data/regional-map.ts` owns geographic selection and field calculations. The approved SVG, coordinate list and original icons are under `src/assets/maps/`, with source/meaning boundaries in its README. The comparison UI under `public/sf-map-lab/` remains separate; fixture weather and simulated positions are not promoted into the app.

**Selected visual language.** Brown elevation bands and green park elevation retain DataSF land/park geometry and USGS-derived terrain. Warm land (`#f0ede1`), pale water (`#dceff5`), the local Atkinson Hyperlegible Next face, red high (`#7e202a`), blue low (`#124577`), gold UV and blue qualifying rain remain grounded in the approved study and incumbent app. The map's styles are component-scoped; global tokens remain authoritative. Highs lead smaller lows, with UV/qualifying rain below and no label backing, dots or leaders. The selected competition freestyle, solid compass and person-pin artwork remains. Roads/bridges are schematic and unnamed; no landmark or north-compass system is introduced.

The integrated responsive type uses container-relative clamps, reaching the selected 24px high / approximately 18.6667px low at full scale rather than imposing the archive's fixed-size rule at every width. All fifteen labels remain displayed. The phone overview can be dense; native point selection and zoom provide additional access. Zoom doubles the map plane within the original-height pannable viewport and centers near the user when available; Show all SF restores the extent. Neither label counts nor overflow checks prove collision-free readability for every possible location or value.

**Real inputs and nearby priority.** All 22 predefined forecast samples use the existing `useLocation` query/repository pipeline. Fifteen distinct labels are selected by geographic separation, reserving the nearest sample first for an in-map user. The twelve former exploration proposals are now registered forecast-sampling coordinates as authorized for this integration; they are not claims of physical sensors, official stations or polygonal neighborhood boundaries. `src/config.ts` registers these samples and four independent beach coordinates in the existing API coordinate allowlist. This is a source integration on the branch, not a claim that it has been deployed.

An existing browser location grant is reused; otherwise the user explicitly selects Use my location. Only successful real location creates a user pin. Unknown, denied and outside-map states use a usable geographic fallback without inventing a position. Location chooses among predefined samples; it does not fabricate forecast coordinates or upload a new arbitrary query point. The map follows the shared forecast date, using the existing observed-completed-hour / forecast-future-hour summary rules and modeled full-day UV peak.

The daily-high field interpolates available real summaries at runtime, rather than loading the study's synthetic gradient PNG. Its legend states the actual contributing sample count, min/max scale and interpolation. Fewer than two valid samples removes the field; missing readings show dashes rather than fixture values. Rain appears strictly above 0.05 daily inches, with `≥` identifying partial totals; wind appears strictly above 15mph. Beach queries keep water temperature and wind independent from neighborhood forecasts and do not manufacture surf estimates or water-quality ratings. Point actions and adjacent-region controls lead to existing regional forecasts.

**Viewport and source limits.** The asset uses `viewBox="0 0 1000 875"`, ending at latitude 37.7091667 north of the known mainland geometry's southern boundary. Land therefore continues through the map window's bottom edge without fabricated extension geometry. Source geometry, schematic bridges/roads, DEM smoothing and coarse-terrain limitations remain documented in the asset metadata and study source notes. The interpolated weather field is a model between sampled forecasts, not a measured continuous weather surface.

**Evidence and delivery boundary.** The [site finish review](review-site/finish-review.md) records SHIP within the reviewed local scope. Its desktop, phone, refreshed phone-zoom and unavailable captures (`site-map-desktop.png`, `site-map-phone.png`, `site-map-phone-zoom.png`, `site-map-unavailable.png`) use controlled schema-valid responses with external providers blocked. This supports integration behavior, not successful live-provider availability or deployed production validation. The review retains limits around accessibility, real devices, performance and all possible label combinations.

The work stays on `codex/sf-regional-map-exploration`; no deployment or merge was performed. These scoped notes do not create global `PRODUCT.md`, `DESIGN.md` or `.impeccable/design.json`; their pre-existing absence remains a separate documentation gap. This pass edits documentation only. ## Geographic and interaction boundaries

`map-engine.js` projects longitude/latitude into a study coordinate plane of 1000 × 810. Ten incumbent SF locations are distinguished from four proposed additions. Forecast points remain at their stored coordinates while authored label offsets move the readable button bodies. Real button hit boxes follow those bodies, with at least 44px label height in the final rules. Compensating anchor-dot positions preserve the point location even when a label moves.

The SVG leader layer is decorative, hidden from assistive technology and ignores pointer input. It connects each visible point to the nearest edge of its displaced label; beach connectors originate at each beach's independently projected coordinate and end at its symbol. Lines are drawn after rendering and recomputed through a `ResizeObserver`. They explain authored offsets, not forecast boundaries or a production collision-avoidance algorithm.

Ocean Beach, Baker Beach, Crissy Field and Aquatic Park have separate coordinates, sample conditions, status words and detail drawers. Selecting a beach does not turn it into a neighborhood forecast or replace the weather selection. Surf, water quality, sample age and missing evidence stay separate. Color is accompanied by words and status shapes; “No posted advisory” must not become “safe.” Every weather and beach value is synthetic, including the deliberately varied data and weather states.

Coverage sketches are illustrative, not validated service polygons. Geography is simplified; source provenance and limitations belong with the study's source documentation. Locate toggles a demo point without geolocation access. Marin and East Bay controls explain the SF boundary rather than navigating to live regional maps. Persistence and JSON export record local comparison choices only; they do not change production preferences.

## Mobile scope

Responsive rules use the named `weather` container, with adjustments at 900px and 620px. At the smaller size the detail dock moves below the map, a region selector keeps every point reachable, and representative labels plus the selected point reduce congestion. The map becomes square while geographic layers occupy the same inset band (8% from the top, 81% high). Authored offsets and leader lines preserve the coordinate relationship. Beach labels yield to symbols; the beach summary and drawer retain the condition wording. Reduced-motion rules disable animations and transitions.

Thumbnail maps are deliberately inert and hidden from assistive technology. They retain a fixed desktop-like map composition and scale as a whole; their tiny text is comparison imagery, not the functional mobile reading surface. The full-size inspector and standalone maps are the interaction surfaces. These density and breakpoint decisions apply to this SF study, not to every dashboard or region.

## Provisional decisions and integration boundaries

Label placement is hand-authored for this geographic extent and fixture set. Long translated names, live error messages, denser regional point sets, different map extents and real provider data still need integration review. The study's responsive behavior and reported browser verification do not establish production data correctness or universal collision handling.

Future integration starts with an explicitly chosen subset of recipes and symbols. Extract the selected map behavior into the existing application architecture; reconcile its local materials with the incumbent tokens deliberately, retain local font licensing, and review new scope against the app's existing typography and interaction patterns. Connect forecast, beach evidence, geolocation, routing and durable preferences only through their actual production contracts. Preserve independent beach coordinates and evidence semantics, keyboard access, geographic anchors and a reachable mobile selection path.
