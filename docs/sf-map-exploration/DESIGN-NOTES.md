# SF map exploration design notes

This records a scoped SF map exploration, not a product-wide design system. Revision 5 is current: the user selected “High leads, low recedes,” the solid teal swimmer disc with a smaller glyph, and all 22 forecast overlays at every width. Other comparison treatments remain available. Revision 4 and earlier sections describe archives, including superseded density and collision policies. Parks-first, softened brown/green elevation, the selected glyphs and the other retained boundaries below remain fixed. The [original review brief](README.md#review-brief-and-design-contract) supplies historical scope.

## Revision 5: selected sizes and all-point visibility

The current [parks-study.html](../../public/sf-map-lab/parks-study.html), `parks-study.js`, `parks-study.css` and `density-choices.js` implement this narrow selection. Revision 4 is preserved as [parks-study-v4.html](../../public/sf-map-lab/parks-study-v4.html), its matching scripts/styles and `density-choices-v4.js`. The remaining comparison options and reversible candidate controls are retained; the initial `high-led` and `filled` recipe now records user selections, not recommendations awaiting selection.

**Exact typography and swimmer scale.** “High leads, low recedes” uses a literal CSS 18pt high and 14pt low, equivalent to 24px and approximately 18.6667px. These fixed sizes apply at every preview width. They use the local WeatherDash Atkinson Hyperlegible Next variable font. The selected solid teal disc keeps its centered 44px target and 34px visible surface; only the white competition freestyle glyph shrinks from 27px to 13.5px, half its former width and height. The button itself is unchanged in size.

**Explicit all-point policy.** All 22 overlays must remain displayed: the original ten and twelve approximate proposed demo locations described under revision 4. `visiblePoints()` returns the full set, the density selector offers only 22, and the new `sf-parks-study-v5` state plus accepted-value checks prevents an older 10/16-point recipe from reducing it. Responsive label hiding, collision classes, `fitMobile` culling and automatic personal-marker suppression are removed. The user can still toggle the demo marker explicitly. Point names remain reachable through the selector and accessible labels.

The user's explicit all-point requirement overrides the earlier representative/collision policy. With unchanged geographic positions and fixed text sizes, narrow maps are intentionally dense and can overlap. Displaying all 22 is not a claim that every reading is unobstructed or universally legible at narrow widths. Do not reintroduce culling or personal-marker suppression as an assumed requirement from the archived revisions.

**Retained scope.** Red high/blue low, dark-gold UV and blue qualifying rain on row two, the strictly-greater-than-0.05-inch daily precipitation rule, absence of leaders/dots, independent beaches, softened elevation, map-caption removals and the tight SF geographic/schematic boundaries remain as documented. The twelve extra coordinates and all weather/beach values remain illustrative; all-point display does not approve production coverage or change provider configuration. Solid compass navigation, person-pin artwork and the central-south simulated location remain unchanged.

**Verification boundary.** The implementation team's check reports 35 assertions passing for exact computed sizes and all 22 displayed overlays at widths 320, 390, 768 and 1440px, plus popup, export and reload behavior. Current captures are under `review-v5`. These checks establish the selected size/count behavior for this fixture, not overlap-free narrow-screen readability or production readiness. The final visual verdict is recorded separately by the implementation/review pass. This documentation changes only these notes; it does not alter UI, assets, main app source, tokens or global design files.

## Revision 4 archive: reopened density and button exploration

The preserved [parks-study-v4.html](../../public/sf-map-lab/parks-study-v4.html), its matching scripts/styles and `density-choices-v4.js` compare six weather treatments and five swimmer-button treatments. The selected revision 3 is preserved as [parks-study-v3.html](../../public/sf-map-lab/parks-study-v3.html) with its corresponding scripts/styles. In revision 4, compact weather and paper-disc defaults were recommendations; revision 5 supersedes them with user-selected high-led weather and filled swimmers. Its all-point policy also supersedes the 10/16-point choices and collision refinement recorded below. Keeping or trying a candidate remains local comparison state, not a product-wide commitment.

**Reopened weather presentation.** The six alternatives are compact bare text (18px high / low), the larger reference (25px), high-led typography (21px high / 15px low), a small paper capsule, a transparent fine outline and paired temperature lozenges. They use the actual locally hosted WeatherDash Atkinson Hyperlegible Next variable face. The user has authorized exploring small surfaces and shapes again; do not restore revision 3's blanket no-background restriction when reviewing or integrating a chosen candidate. Smaller readings test density, not a new app-wide type scale.

The information contract remains daily high / low in red/blue on row one, UV maximum and qualifying rain on row two, with precipitation shown only when daily inches are strictly greater than 0.05. No weather leader lines or point dots return. Values remain tied to their projected coordinates. Captions, region names inside weather labels, standalone north indicators and landmark icons remain absent from the map surface; accessible names, the point selector and surrounding context retain orientation.

**Proposed point coverage.** The 22-point fixture combines the ten incumbent SF locations with twelve approximate proposed demo locations: Presidio, Haight-Ashbury, Castro, Noe Valley, Glen Park, Bernal Heights, Potrero Hill, Mission Bay, Bayview, Excelsior, West Portal and Ingleside. These are illustrative sampling proposals with invented weather values, not added service coverage, approved regional boundaries, verified station sites or provider integrations. No main `src/config` change is implied. The 10 / 16 / 22 density control compares the original set, an intermediate set and all proposals; the intermediate set adds Noe Valley, Glen Park, Potrero Hill, Bayview, Excelsior and West Portal. The interface distinguishes added locations and reports counts. Hidden mobile representatives do not change the underlying demo dataset; the selector keeps the active density set reachable.

**Swimmer affordance.** The competition freestyle glyph remains fixed. Five alternatives change its surrounding button: paper disc, bare glyph, filled teal disc, paper squircle and outline ring. All use centered 44px button targets at the independent beach coordinates. The appearance comparison does not reopen the chosen swimmer artwork or merge beach evidence with neighborhood forecasts. Solid compass navigation (`compass-solid`) and person-pin (`person-pin`) remain fixed. The simulated person moves to longitude -122.448, latitude 37.730 to avoid the new demo readings; this is a fixture position without geolocation access.

**Softer terrain and retained geography.** The brown/green elevation system is retained at 60 / 120 / 180 / 240m. Each displayed band color blends 70% of its revision 3 band with 30% of its matching base (land `#f0ede1`, parks `#dce7ce`), calculated per RGB channel. This softens contrast without changing contour geometry, elevation thresholds or source provenance. The tight revision 3 bounds, shared reprojection, caption removals, clipped bridge spans, mirrored Golden Gate structure and schematic road limitations remain in force. The terrain is still derived from the local coarse USGS raster, with DataSF land/park geometry; it is not a newly surveyed map.

**Mobile collision refinement.** Non-selected representative weather yields to fixed beach controls and the visible simulated personal marker. The selected reading stays readable. When the simulated personal marker would obscure the selected reading, it is temporarily hidden with an explanatory note outside the map, retaining its geographic coordinate. The swimmer crop excludes partial weather rows at its lower boundary while keeping all three northern beaches. These are bounded prototype rules, not a production collision engine.

**Comparison and persistence.** Revision 4 uses independent `sf-parks-study-v4` browser state for its recipe and kept candidates. Accepted choices include weather style, swimmer-button style, density, fixture day, selected point and demo-location visibility; the fixed terrain system and glyph selections are not reopened by old recipe values. Minimize, hide and restore are reversible presentation controls. Comparison decks switch to carousels when needed, and candidate/location counts make the active scope visible. Persisted or exported selections belong to this local exploration and do not update production preferences or coverage.

**Verification and integration boundary.** The implementation team's current check reports 83 browser assertions passing, weather contrast of at least 5.8:1 against the evaluated fills and 8.6:1 for the filled swimmer treatment, with no browser errors or external requests. Those are scoped fixture checks; the fresh finish review marked both bounded mobile/crop findings resolved and approved the demo exploration. That review is recorded in `review-v4/finish-review.md`; it does not certify production integration. A future integration should consume the user's eventual weather/button selections alongside the retained decisions, reconcile study-local styles with incumbent tokens, and connect real forecast, beach and navigation contracts deliberately. It must not treat the twelve proposals as approved production sampling sites. This pass changes only these notes; no UI, assets, main app source or durable global design files are modified. The previously noted global-documentation gap remains separate.

## Revision 3 archive: selected map and implementation boundary

The preserved [parks-study-v3.html](../../public/sf-map-lab/parks-study-v3.html), `parks-study-v3.js` and `parks-study-v3.css` implement this historical selection. Revision 4 reopens its weather presentation and swimmer-button appearance and softens the elevation materials. The following section records revision 3, not additional constraints on those reopened choices. Revision 2 is preserved as `parks-study-v2.*`; the original comparison is also archived. No revision changes the main WeatherDash application or its global tokens.

**Pinned materials and symbols.** Elevation bands at 60 / 120 / 180 / 240m use warm brown outside park overlap and green within it. Land-clipped brown bands are covered by the park base and park-clipped green bands, so elevation retains a consistent height structure while parks remain distinct. The chosen icons are the original competition freestyle swimmer (`freestyle`), solid compass needle (`compass-solid`) for adjacent regions, and person in a pin (`person-pin`) for simulated personal location. These replace the earlier open icon and terrain choices.

**Bare weather.** Daily high / low values form the first row, red (`#7e202a`) and blue (`#124577`) with a slash separator. Daily UV maximum in dark gold (`#5c3e00`) and qualifying daily precipitation in blue (`#064860`) share the second row. The existing rule remains strictly greater than 0.05 inches; exactly 0.05 stays hidden. Weather backgrounds, halos, shadows, leader lines and point dots are removed in all states, including hover, selected and keyboard focus. Focus retains its visible outline; selection increases temperature weight. Values stay centered on their forecast coordinates rather than being detached callouts.

The four weather colors were checked against the actual base land, park and all eight elevation-band fills. Each has a minimum contrast above 4.9:1 in that set (lowest approximately 4.92:1). This scopes the measurement to the implemented terrain/park fills; it is not a claim about every possible future background or every UI component. Labels use the incumbent local Atkinson Hyperlegible Next variable asset, with the existing bold Atkinson face for the wordmark.

**Text and independent beaches.** Within the map surface, visible text is restricted to weather readings and the Marin / East Bay destination controls. Park, bridge, water, road and region captions are removed; the standalone north indicator remains absent. Names and units stay available outside the surface, in accessible labels and in the mobile selector. Swimmer symbols remain directly on their separate beach coordinates without labels or leaders. Their dialogs retain independent sample beach evidence and preserve the selected forecast point. Forecast and beach data remains illustrative.

**Viewport and schematic structures.** The selected bounds are west -122.522, east -122.351, north 37.820 and south 37.706, projected into the existing 1000 × 900 plane. This tight mainland SF composition clips bridge spans at its edges. The old USGS-derived contour paths are reprojected from their larger source extent into the new viewport, rather than regenerated as invented terrain or stretched independently of geography. The source raster, smoothing and coarse visualization limits documented under revision 2 still apply. DataSF land/park provenance remains; the current mainland land path does not add the prior schematic Marin headland or island layer.

Golden Gate Bridge's structure is mirrored across its deck while its deck and road alignment remain fixed. Bay Bridge and I-80 connections remain visible where the crop permits. Bridge drawings and road lines are schematic orientation geometry, not surveyed structures or routing data. The simulated person marker is placed at longitude -122.435, latitude 37.743 in an unoccupied central-south position so it does not cover a forecast; it does not report the user's actual location.

**State and mobile boundary.** The current study uses separate `sf-parks-study-v3` local storage. Only fixture day (`sample`), selected forecast point (`selected`) and simulated-location visibility (`located`) can vary through accepted storage/query values. Visual selections are pinned in source and cannot be replaced by old recipe values or query parameters. At the 650px `selectedmap` container breakpoint, representative weather labels plus the selected point reduce density while the selector keeps every forecast point reachable. All layers retain the shared projection. This revision does not inherit revision 2's displaced labels, leader recalculation or square-map inset behavior.

**Future integration.** Preserve these confirmed SF choices when extracting the mockup into WeatherDash. Reconcile local styling with the existing token and component sources deliberately; connect real forecast, beach, routing and location contracts separately. Real data, different geographic extents and longer accessible content still require implementation review. This selected surface does not authorize a new product-wide design system, global `PRODUCT.md`, `DESIGN.md` or `.impeccable/design.json`, or edits to incumbent app code. Missing global design documentation remains a pre-existing gap. This pass documents boundaries only and makes no new production-validation claim.

## Revision 2 archive: parks-first exploration

The preserved artifact is [parks-study-v2.html](../../public/sf-map-lab/parks-study-v2.html), supported by the archived `parks-study-v2` files and shared `parks-icons.js`. The [revision record](../../public/sf-map-lab/REVISION.md) captures that revision's changes. The following section describes its historical state; revision 3 supersedes its formerly open choices and map-caption decisions.

**Confirmed direction.** Parks supply the orientation structure. Golden Gate Bridge and Bay Bridge appear along their crossings, with connecting roads including I-80 across Bay Bridge. Road names and route numbers are omitted. Landmark icons and the standalone north compass are removed. Park names remain; bridge captions identify the two requested features. Compasses belong beside adjacent-region destination actions, not on the map as north indicators.

**Forecast content.** Map labels contain daily temperature high / low, daily UV maximum and precipitation only when the daily total is strictly greater than 0.05 inches. Exactly 0.05 is hidden. Inches of daily total is the documented interpretation of the threshold, not a production provider change. Region names, current readings, wind, sky symbols, rain probability and time phrases are absent inside the labels. Names remain in accessible button labels, the mobile selector and selected-point footer. The four appearances—compact ticket, horizontal ribbon, three-column reading and open type—hold the facts constant and remain alternatives.

**Typography and materials.** Revision 2 uses the same local Atkinson Hyperlegible Next variable font asset as WeatherDash body and data text; the wordmark uses the incumbent bold Atkinson Hyperlegible face. This supersedes the original study's static regular/bold body family only on the new mockup. Warm land (`#f0ede1`) and pale water (`#dceff5`) remain inherited from the SD map. The new shell uses WeatherDash's warm page (`#fbf1df`), surface (`#fff8eb`), ink (`#2d2018`) and muted ink (`#684a35`) as local declarations. Main app tokens and styles remain unchanged; neither study stylesheet is an app-wide replacement.

**Beach and icon behavior.** Swimmers sit directly on the four independent beach coordinates without visible map labels, detached callouts or beach leader lines. Accessible beach names and beach-specific dialogs retain context; opening a beach preserves the selected weather point. All weather and beach values remain synthetic. The SVG-only lists provide five swimmers, five compasses and six personal-location images (a crosshair plus five people), each shown at three sizes with individual SVG downloads. The solid competition freestyle swimmer is original artwork in the requested Olympic-style direction, not official Olympic artwork. Selecting an icon is a local preview preference; a personal-location choice reveals a simulated marker without geolocation or heading access. Specific SVG variants remain provisional.

**Terrain provenance and limits.** Five alternatives share one real elevation source: shaded relief, elevation bands, sparse contours, full topographic contours and a flat reference. Four are derived terrain treatments, not four independently invented hill patterns. `docs/sf-map-exploration/prepare-terrain.py` transforms the local `data/sf-elevation-usgs.tiff`, a public USGS 3DEP export covering longitude -122.544 to -122.348 and latitude 37.707 to 37.846 at 360 × 324 samples. It clamps negative elevations to zero, smooths twice, simplifies derived curves, and creates a source-metadata-bearing hillshade PNG and 20m contour geometry. Sparse contours use 80m intervals; bands use 60 / 120 / 180 / 240m steps. Terrain selection is open. These coarse, styled derivatives support city-scale orientation, not surveyed heights or navigation. The browser uses local assets without a live elevation, tile or font service.

Land, parks and restored island geometry retain DataSF provenance. The small Marin headland is authored schematic geometry. Bridge structures are original vector drawings and road alignments are simplified, including their joining geometry and continued connections toward the viewport edge. Their connected appearance conveys orientation; it does not establish engineering accuracy, lane layout, route accessibility or a routing graph.

**Placement and mobile scope.** Revision 2 projects the expanded extent into a 1000 × 900 plane. Weather coordinates remain fixed while authored label offsets and decorative leaders preserve their relationship. Beaches stay on their own coordinates. The named `newmap` container adapts at 650px: the map becomes square, geographic and point layers share 90% height, and representative weather labels yield to the selected point while all points stay reachable through the picker. Narrower adjustments reduce congestion. Fixed-composition thumbnails remain inert comparisons. These are hand-authored placements for this fixture set, not a general collision solver; live content and other extents require fresh review.

**Integration boundary.** Parks-first and the listed content/removal decisions are selected for this SF direction. Terrain treatment, label appearance and individual swimmer, compass and personal-location drawings still need selection. Recipe storage uses its own `sf-parks-study-v2` key and export, separate from the archive. Extraction into WeatherDash should preserve confirmed semantics while reconciling local declarations with existing tokens and connecting real data, navigation and location contracts deliberately. The main app, providers and global design files are untouched. This documentation does not declare review findings resolved or claim a new production validation pass.

## Inherited evidence

The visual reference is the rendered SD map in `sdbf/src/map.js`: warm land (`#f0ede1`) and pale blue water (`#dceff5`). The SF study preserves those exact fills in `public/sf-map-lab/map.css`. Fine roads, quiet orientation captions, coordinate-based points and compact readable type carry forward the map's hierarchy. The SD renderer uses lighter road casing (`#fffdf6`) with route strokes (`#849098` and `#9ca5a1`); SF's simplified street network uses its own study-local strokes rather than claiming identical geography or rendering.

`sdbf/preview/preview.css` supplies additional interaction evidence: selected pins, accompanying evidence panels, visible keyboard focus, status words and colors, and a map/list arrangement that stacks on small screens. Its surrounding preview shell uses system type; the production SD map explicitly names Atkinson Hyperlegible. Those are distinct sources, not a single shared token system.

The study loads local Atkinson Hyperlegible regular and bold TTF files from `public/sf-map-lab/assets/`, with the copied OFL license alongside them. Its CSS aliases the family as `Atkinson`, maps regular to weights 400–600 and bold to 650–900, uses `font-display: swap`, and disables synthesized faces. These are two static font files, not a variable font. The incumbent dashboard separately uses local Atkinson Hyperlegible bold for headings and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next variable for body text in `src/styles.css`; its typography remains authoritative for that app.

The main app's `src/design-tokens.css` remains unchanged. Its warm Sunline page (`#fbf1df`), ink (`#2d2018`), orange accent (`#e85d32`) and water surface (`#d8eef4`) are incumbent app values, not values to overwrite with this study's map palette. The root has a pre-existing gap in global `PRODUCT.md`, `DESIGN.md` and `.impeccable/design.json` documentation. ## Geographic and interaction boundaries

`map-engine.js` projects longitude/latitude into a study coordinate plane of 1000 × 810. Ten incumbent SF locations are distinguished from four proposed additions. Forecast points remain at their stored coordinates while authored label offsets move the readable button bodies. Real button hit boxes follow those bodies, with at least 44px label height in the final rules. Compensating anchor-dot positions preserve the point location even when a label moves.

The SVG leader layer is decorative, hidden from assistive technology and ignores pointer input. It connects each visible point to the nearest edge of its displaced label; beach connectors originate at each beach's independently projected coordinate and end at its symbol. Lines are drawn after rendering and recomputed through a `ResizeObserver`. They explain authored offsets, not forecast boundaries or a production collision-avoidance algorithm.

Ocean Beach, Baker Beach, Crissy Field and Aquatic Park have separate coordinates, sample conditions, status words and detail drawers. Selecting a beach does not turn it into a neighborhood forecast or replace the weather selection. Surf, water quality, sample age and missing evidence stay separate. Color is accompanied by words and status shapes; “No posted advisory” must not become “safe.” Every weather and beach value is synthetic, including the deliberately varied data and weather states.

Coverage sketches are illustrative, not validated service polygons. Geography is simplified; source provenance and limitations belong with the study's source documentation. Locate toggles a demo point without geolocation access. Marin and East Bay controls explain the SF boundary rather than navigating to live regional maps. Persistence and JSON export record local comparison choices only; they do not change production preferences.

## Mobile scope

Responsive rules use the named `weather` container, with adjustments at 900px and 620px. At the smaller size the detail dock moves below the map, a region selector keeps every point reachable, and representative labels plus the selected point reduce congestion. The map becomes square while geographic layers occupy the same inset band (8% from the top, 81% high). Authored offsets and leader lines preserve the coordinate relationship. Beach labels yield to symbols; the beach summary and drawer retain the condition wording. Reduced-motion rules disable animations and transitions.

Thumbnail maps are deliberately inert and hidden from assistive technology. They retain a fixed desktop-like map composition and scale as a whole; their tiny text is comparison imagery, not the functional mobile reading surface. The full-size inspector and standalone maps are the interaction surfaces. These density and breakpoint decisions apply to this SF study, not to every dashboard or region.

## Provisional decisions and integration boundaries

Label placement is hand-authored for this geographic extent and fixture set. Long translated names, live error messages, denser regional point sets, different map extents and real provider data still need integration review. The study's responsive behavior and reported browser verification do not establish production data correctness or universal collision handling.

Future integration starts with an explicitly chosen subset of recipes and symbols. Extract the selected map behavior into the existing application architecture; reconcile its local materials with the incumbent tokens deliberately, retain local font licensing, and review new scope against the app's existing typography and interaction patterns. Connect forecast, beach evidence, geolocation, routing and durable preferences only through their actual production contracts. Preserve independent beach coordinates and evidence semantics, keyboard access, geographic anchors and a reachable mobile selection path.

This documentation pass changes only this file. It does not alter UI, generated assets, app tokens, providers, database, routing or the SD beach finder. Global design documentation remains a separate future task after durable product-wide decisions exist.
