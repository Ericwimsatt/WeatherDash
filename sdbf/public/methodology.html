How Beach Finder scores water quality

The safety score runs from 1 to 10, with 1 for the lowest observed concern and 10 for dangerous conditions. It describes the evidence available for the named monitoring point. Conditions can change between samples and along the beach.

ScoreMeaning
1–2Lowest observed concern
3–4Use caution
5–6Sensitive groups should avoid
7–8Avoid water contact
9–10Dangerous. Avoid water contact.

What raises the score

  • County postings and verified spills. A County closure or warning, or a verified spill affecting the site, scores 10. A water-quality advisory scores at least 9. A confirmed rain-only advisory uses the rain scale. The published estimate of a lift stays informational until the County updates its postings.
  • Rain and runoff. Crossing the local runoff limit of 0.20 inches or the four-day inland limit of 0.75 inches sets a score of at least 6. Local totals of 0.35 inches or inland totals of 1.00 inch set 7. Heavy rain of at least 0.50 inches locally or 1.25 inches inland can score 8 by itself. An official Countywide rain advisory sets a minimum of 7 along the full coast until it is lifted. The runoff thresholds and the 1–10 scale are app rules.
  • Regional rainfall. Beach Finder uses the highest 72-hour coastal total among monitored beaches in each region (North County, Central Coast, or South County). Coastal rain of at least 0.05, 0.20, or 0.35 inches adds 1, 2, or 3 points respectively to each beach in that region. The adjustment can raise a score to 8. Higher scores set by bacteria or other hazards still apply.
  • Bacteria and sample age. A result at or above the method’s limit in the past 72 hours scores at least 9. A result at twice the limit, or a recent exceedance with qualifying local rainfall, scores 10. If the last test exceeded the limit more than 72 hours ago, that result sets a minimum score of 6 until a newer test is available. An older below-limit result or an unavailable sample adds no points. A recent result near the limit can increase the score.
  • County status checks. If either the County beach posting or the Countywide rain advisory status is unverified, the base score is at least 5. If both are unverified, it is at least 6.

With current County status and no negative indicators, the score starts at 1. The strongest site-specific signal or County advisory sets the base score, then the regional rainfall adjustment applies. A low reading, a dry gauge, or a County open status cannot cancel another hazard. Enterococcus limits are 1,413 DNA copies per 100 mL for ddPCR and 104 organisms per 100 mL for culture. Reported bounds such as <195 retain their original qualifier.

Rainfall and runoff

Local rain covers the last 72 hours. Inland rain covers the last 96 hours at a point about three miles east of each beach station. The four-day total also shares rain from other inland points within five miles. For each hour, the beach keeps its own reading or uses a higher nearby reading adjusted toward its own. The closer the point, the more its rain counts. At five miles its influence reaches zero. The app counts the highest adjusted value for each hour. If the beach’s own point has no value for an hour, the nearest available point within five miles supplies the starting value.

Rainfall comes from Open-Meteo’s coordinate series, with NWS gauges supplying additional coastal readings. Forecast hours appear on the runoff chart and stay outside the current rating.
