How Beach Finder scores water quality

The safety score runs from 1 to 10, with 1 for low risk and 10 for extremely high risk of infection if you enter the water. It describes the evidence available for the named monitoring point. Conditions can change between samples and along the beach.

ScoreMeaning
1–2Low risk
3–4Sensitive groups should use caution
5–6Sensitive groups should avoid
7–8Everyone should avoid water contact
9–10Extremely high risk of infection if you enter the water

What raises the score

  • County status and verified spills. A local County advisory adds 6 points, a warning adds 8, and a closure adds 10, capped at a total score of 10. A verified spill affecting the site scores 10. A confirmed rain-only advisory uses the rain scale. The published estimate of a lift stays informational until the County updates its status.
  • Nearby County notices. A local advisory, warning, or closure at another monitored beach contributes 70% of its penalty within 2 miles, 25% over 2 and up to 4 miles, and 0% beyond 4 miles (including 4–6 miles). Distance is measured directly between monitoring points. We scale the additive penalty (+6 for advisories, +8 for warnings, +10 for closures), round to whole points, and add it after other factors and regional rainfall, capped at 10. A nearby advisory adds 4 points within 2 miles or 2 points within 4 miles. Only the strongest direct or nearby County penalty applies; overlapping notices do not stack or spread onward from neighboring beaches. Last-known restrictions remain until updated. Rain-only advisories use the separate rain rules. This distance adjustment is an app rule, not a County advisory for the neighboring beach.
  • Rain and runoff. Local rain over 72 hours scores 2 at 0.03 inches, 4 at 0.20 inches, 6 at 0.35 inches, and 8 at 0.50 inches. Inland rain over four days scores 3 at 0.35 inches, 4 at 0.75 inches, 5 at 1.00 inch, and 6 at 1.25 inches. An official Countywide rain advisory sets a minimum of 7 along the full coast until it is lifted. The runoff thresholds and the 1–10 scale are app rules.
  • Regional rainfall. Beach Finder uses the highest available 72-hour coastal total among monitored beaches in each region (North County, Central Coast, or South County). Coastal rain of at least 0.12 or 0.35 inches adds 2 or 3 points respectively to each beach in that region. Totals below 0.12 inches add no regional points. The adjustment can raise a score to 8. Higher scores set by bacteria or other hazards still apply.
  • Bacteria and sample age. We check all comparable Enterococcus tests collected in the past 96 hours (4 days), using each test method’s own advisory limit. The highest concern sets the bacteria score: at least 10% of the limit scores 2, 25% scores 3 and 40% scores 4 (sensitive groups should use caution), and 75% scores 5 (sensitive groups should avoid water contact). A result at or above the limit scores at least 9; twice the limit, or an exceedance with qualifying local rain, scores 10. A newer low result does not erase a higher result within those 4 days. If the latest test exceeded the limit more than 4 days ago, it sets a minimum of 6 until a newer test is available. Older below-limit results and unavailable samples add no points. Results reported as upper bounds, such as <195, do not establish an elevated concentration.
  • County status checks. If either the County beach posting or the Countywide rain advisory status is unverified, the base score is at least 5. If both are unverified, it is at least 6.

With current County status and no negative indicators, the score starts at 1. The strongest site-specific signal or County rain advisory sets the base score, then the regional rainfall adjustment applies, followed by the strongest local or nearby County penalty. A low reading, a dry gauge, or a County open status cannot cancel another hazard. Enterococcus limits are 1,413 DNA copies per 100 mL for ddPCR and 104 organisms per 100 mL for culture. Reported bounds such as <195 retain their original qualifier.

What bacteria tests mean

Enterococcus is a sign that human or animal waste may be in the water. Some programs also test coliform bacteria. These tests signal that germs may be present, including viruses and bacteria that can cause stomach illness, rashes, or ear and breathing infections.

The County’s ddPCR test counts bacterial DNA and usually provides same-day results. Its advisory level is 1,413 DNA copies per 100 mL. Culture tests grow live bacteria and use 104 MPN or CFU per 100 mL. Compare each reading with its own method’s limit; the numbers and units are not interchangeable.

A reading at 25% of the advisory level triggers “Bacteria levels higher than normal” and a caution score. Swimming may still be possible below the limit, but some risk remains. This is an app rule, not a measured local average or a County advisory. Follow current County postings.

Rainfall and runoff

Local rain covers the past 72 hours. Inland rain covers the past 96 hours at a point about three miles east of each beach station. Nearby inland locations have more influence on the inland estimate. Runoff warnings describe possible effects of rain, including pollutants carried through storm drains, creeks, and rivers.

The chart uses hourly NWS rain gauge observations when available. If a point has no recent gauge readings, its past rainfall falls back to the coordinate-based estimate. Unreported hours remain blank, so past totals may be incomplete. Open-Meteo supplies the 10-day forecast. Forecast rain appears on the chart but does not affect the current rating. Dashed guides mark each local, inland, and regional scoring threshold. The rain outlook uses these same rules, including regional points, to estimate when rain-related scores fall to 1–2. It cannot predict when bacteria, spills, or unverified County status will clear.
